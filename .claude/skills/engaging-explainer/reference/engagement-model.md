# The engagement model

The research this format rests on, and the rules that follow from it. Read this
before writing. Several findings are counterintuitive enough that instinct gets
them backwards.

---

## 1. Curiosity needs a SMALL gap

**Loewenstein's information-gap theory (1994):** curiosity is a state of
*deprivation* — it arises when attention focuses on a gap in one's knowledge.
The critical, counterintuitive part:

> **The size of the gap predicts curiosity intensity — but inversely. Large gaps
> produce low curiosity; small gaps produce high curiosity.** And curiosity
> requires prior knowledge: *we are not curious about something we know
> absolutely nothing about.*

### What follows

- **Never open on a definition.** "What is an agentic harness?" is a large gap
  for someone who has never heard the term — so it produces *low* curiosity, not
  high. It feels like homework.
- **Seed, then open.** Give the viewer something they already know, then reveal
  the small missing piece adjacent to it. "You already know the model. Here's why
  the same model feels completely different in two products" is a small gap.
- **Gaps must be closable.** The viewer has to believe five minutes will close
  it. A gap that feels like a semester's work is dismissed, not pursued.

### The seed-then-open test

Before every gap in the script, ask: *what does the viewer already know that
makes this feel adjacent?* If the answer is "nothing", you have opened a large
gap and lost them. Add the seed.

## 2. The first 3 seconds, and the 30-second cliff

Retention curves drop steepest in the first 15–30 seconds while casual viewers
decide whether to commit, then decline gradually. **Holding 70%+ at 0:30 is the
benchmark for a strong video.**

The drop is usually caused by the promise of the title not being fulfilled
early enough.

### What follows

- **Something must happen by 0:03.** Not "be established" — *happen.*
- **The turn lands by 0:12.** The turn is the moment the premise breaks or
  inverts. If it lands after 0:15, the structure is wrong and no amount of
  visual polish recovers it.
- **Visual velocity floor:** in the first 30 seconds the frame must change
  measurably at least every ~2 seconds. Not scene changes — *changes*: a mark
  arriving, a value moving, something stopping.

---

## 3. Attention is captured by QUALITATIVE motion change

From the visual-attention literature: motion transients capture attention
bottom-up and involuntarily. But specifically —

> **Qualitative changes (commencement or cessation of motion) are what trigger
> attentional capture. Quantitative changes (acceleration, deceleration) do
> not.**

A **hard stop captures attention as powerfully as a hard start.** This is the
most under-used device in explainer video, and it is free.

Related: the **motion silencing effect** — the ability to detect feature changes
in a moving object *decreases sharply with its speed*. Detail changes on
fast-moving elements are literally not perceived.

### What follows

- **Every attention reset must be an onset or a cessation.** Never a ramp.
  A density that gradually increases is a quantitative change and does almost
  nothing; the same density arriving in one hit, or freezing dead, does the work.
- **THE FREEZE DEVICE.** On each key claim, stop *everything* for 300–400ms.
  Total stillness in a moving frame is an attention event. Use it at every act
  boundary and at the peak.
- **Never reveal a detail while the frame is moving.** Numbers, labels and
  fine marks land only in a declared *still window*. If the camera is pushing,
  the viewer cannot read the number you just landed.

## 4. Mayer's multimedia principles — the effect sizes

Measured effect sizes, largest first:

| Principle | d | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Redundancy** | **0.87** | People learn better from graphics + narration than from graphics + narration + on-screen text |
| **Coherence** | **0.70** | Excluding extraneous material improves learning |
| **Signaling** | **0.46** | Cues highlighting the organisation of the material improve learning |

### What follows

- **Redundancy is the biggest measured harm available to you, and captions are
  where it happens.** Verbatim subtitles duplicating narration *reduce*
  comprehension. But most viewing is muted, so captions cannot simply be
  dropped. The resolution:
  - Captions stay, but styled **recessive** — smaller, lower contrast, visually
    ignorable by a viewer who is listening.
  - **Display text never duplicates narration.** If the voice says a number, the
    screen shows the *object*; if the screen shows the number, the voice moves on.
    One channel per fact at a time.
  - The linter checks this: any 4+ word sequence shared between display text and
    narration within ±2s is a redundancy ERROR.
- **Coherence:** every decorative element is a measured cost, not a neutral one.
  If it does not carry meaning, delete it.
- **Signaling:** carry a persistent structural cue — an act spine, a progress
  indicator, a recurring anchor — so the viewer always knows where they are.

## 5. Pattern interrupts, on a progressive rhythm

The brain habituates to repetitive stimuli; interrupts reset attention.
Recommended cadence for long-form:

- **Every 10–20 seconds** for roughly the first 3 minutes.
- **Widening to 25–40 seconds** once the audience is committed.

### What follows

- A scene change is **not** an interrupt if the visual subject stays the same.
  Seven consecutive scenes showing the same room is *one* visual, not seven
  interrupts.
- Interrupt types must vary: a vantage change, a scale jump, a freeze, a colour
  event, a sound event. Rotating through them prevents habituation to the
  interrupts themselves.

---

## 6. Peak-end rule

Memory of an episode is dominated by **the emotional peak and the ending**. The
middle largely vanishes, and **duration is almost entirely ignored.**

### What follows

- **Engineer exactly one peak.** Two or three medium peaks is the common failure
  and it is strictly worse than one large one. The peak should be simultaneously:
  - the **largest scale** in the piece,
  - the only moment at **full saturation**,
  - a **total freeze**, and
  - the loudest point of the sound design.
- **The peak sits at 70–80%** of runtime, not at the end — the ending needs its
  own distinct beat.
- **Do not fade out quietly.** A quiet ending is a legitimate aesthetic choice,
  but it costs half your memorability. Choose it knowingly, and give the last
  frame a single strong idea.

---

## 7. Arousal drives sharing — calm does not

Berger & Milkman: virality is partly driven by **physiological arousal**.
High-arousal emotions — **awe, surprise, anger, anxiety** — increase
transmission. **Low-arousal emotions, including calm and sadness, decrease it**,
even controlling for how interesting or useful the content is.

### What follows

- **"Warm, calm, unhurried" is a low-arousal register.** It reads as tasteful,
  and it measurably suppresses sharing. This is a real trade, not a free choice.
- Treat arousal as a **dial, not a constant.** A piece can be calm at rest and
  spike hard at the peak: contrast, saturation, motion speed and sound all ramp
  together for 3–5 seconds, then release.
- **Awe and surprise are the two high-arousal emotions available to an explainer**
  (anger and anxiety are usually the wrong register). Design the peak to produce
  one of them — usually via *scale revelation* (a thing turning out to be far
  bigger than it looked) or *inversion* (a thing turning out to be its opposite).

## Beat map

Timings scale proportionally with runtime; the windows below are for a
5-minute piece.

| Slot | Window | Job | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **COLD OPEN** | 0:00–0:03 | One arresting frame. Motion **onset**. | `cold_open` |
| **SEED** | 0:03–0:08 | Establish the known thing the gap hangs off. | `gap_seeded` |
| **GAP** | 0:08–0:12 | Name the small missing piece. | — |
| **TURN** | by 0:12 | Qualitative motion event; premise breaks. | `turn_timing` |
| **ACT RUN** | 0:12 → ~75% | Interrupts every 10–20s, widening to 25–40s. | `interrupt_cadence` |
| **PEAK** | 70–80% | One engineered maximum: scale + saturation + freeze + sound. | `single_peak` |
| **RELEASE** | after peak | Drop back hard. Contrast makes the peak. | — |
| **END** | last 8s | One strong idea. Not a fade. | `strong_end` |

---

## The checklist

Before the scene_plan gate:

- [ ] Something *happens* by 0:03.
- [ ] Every gap is seeded before it is opened.
- [ ] The turn lands by 0:12.
- [ ] Frame changes at least every ~2s for the first 30s.
- [ ] Every attention reset is an onset, a cessation, or a freeze — never a ramp.
- [ ] No detail lands while the frame is moving.
- [ ] No display text duplicates narration within ±2s.
- [ ] Exactly one peak, at 70–80%, maxed on scale + saturation + freeze + sound.
- [ ] The ending is a statement, not a fade.
- [ ] The arousal register was chosen deliberately, and its cost is understood.
